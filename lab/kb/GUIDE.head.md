# MicroFreak sound guide, from measurement

This file is generated by `lab/freak guide` from the JSON files beside it. Do not edit it by hand: edit
`GUIDE.head.md` (this introduction) or re-run the measurements.

Everything below was measured on the VibeFreak emulator running Arturia's own firmware 5.0.0.2084, by
setting controls, playing a note and analysing the recording. Settings are given the way the instrument's
screen words them, because the screen text was read off the emulated display.

## How much to trust each part

- **Oscillator types: exact.** The emulator runs the real firmware, and the oscillator is computed entirely
  in that firmware. What is measured here is the signal the real instrument sends to its converter.
- **Envelope and filter-envelope timing: exact in shape, read from the firmware's control outputs.** The
  attack times agree with the times the firmware prints on its screen.
- **Filter: a model.** On the instrument the filter is an analog circuit. The emulator stands in for it with
  a textbook 2-pole state-variable filter. Its cutoff follows the frequency the firmware displays for each
  knob position; how sharp the resonance gets and how the real circuit overdrives are guesses. Use the filter
  numbers for direction (darker, brighter, more peak), not as exact values.
- **Modulation matrix, LFO and cycling envelope: exact.** How far each amount moves each destination, what
  each source puts out, LFO shapes and rates and cycling envelope times are read from the firmware's control
  signals and parameter values. What a modulated *filter* then sounds like still goes through the filter model.
- **Not covered:** Sample, Scan Grains, Cloud Grains and Hit Grains (they play Arturia's factory samples,
  which are not in the firmware download), the Vocoder (needs a microphone), arpeggiator patterns and the
  sequencer (only the arpeggiator's clock was checked), paraphonic mode, the Rise and Fall shape settings of the
  cycling envelope, and the graded pressure of the real touch keyboard. The lab can press those buttons but
  nothing has been measured.
- The emulator's built-in presets are placeholders written by its author, not Arturia's factory presets.

## The measures

All spectrum measures come from about 0.7 s of the held note, starting 120 ms in.

- **brightness centre (harmonic number)**: where the energy sits on average, counted in harmonics of the note.
  A sine is 1.0, a triangle about 1.05, a square about 2.3, a full sawtooth about 3.4. Above 6 is very bright.
- **share of energy on the note's harmonics**: 1.0 is a clean pitched tone. Detuned stacks, chords, noise and
  bell-like clangour pull it down.
- **share of energy in the fundamental**: near 1.0 is close to a sine; near 0 means the note's own pitch is
  nearly absent and the ear hears the upper partials.
- **odd vs even harmonics (dB)**: strongly positive is the hollow, clarinet or square kind of tone; strongly
  negative means the second harmonic leads and the sound leans an octave up.
- **share of energy below the note**: a sub-octave or rumble under the played pitch.
- **loudness wobble while held**: 0 is rock steady; above about 0.12 is audible beating or tremolo. On types that
  die away by themselves (KarplusStr, Modal) this figure mostly reflects the decay, not beating.
- **harmonics 1-8**: level of each harmonic in dB under the strongest one.
- The words (dark, bright, hollow, noisy and so on) are fixed thresholds on these numbers, listed in
  `lab/freaklab/analyze.py`.

## Turning a request into settings

1. Translate the request into measures: "warm" is a low brightness centre with a strong fundamental; "hollow"
   is a high odd/even figure; "glassy" or "bell" is a low harmonic share with a bright centre; "fat" is beating
   plus weight below the note.
2. `lab/freak find` searches the measured settings by word or by number; the per-type tables below show which
   knob moves which measure.
3. `lab/freak play ...` renders a candidate and measures it, including the filter and envelope. Listen to the
   WAV, compare numbers, adjust, repeat.
4. Movement is modulation: vibrato is LFO > Pitch at an amount of 0.5 to 2; a filter wobble is LFO > Cutoff
   at 5 to 15; a pluck that opens the filter is the Filter Amount knob; a sound that brightens as you play
   harder is Press > Cutoff with Press Mode on Velocity; a filter that follows the keyboard is Key/Arp > Cutoff
   at 100. The modulation section below has the scales.
5. Hand over the "On the panel" block from `freak play`: it is in the instrument's own terms.

Knob percentages are of the knob's travel: 0 is hard left (7 o'clock), 50 is straight up, 100 is hard right
(5 o'clock). Wave, Timbre and Shape are endless encoders, so use the screen value for those.
